# Cleo commissions a correction and receives it back

**Buyer disposition: hold the unchanged ZIP.** Arden fixed the three exposed vias and the regulator footprint. Cleo returned Arden's exact file, with a buyer dispatch telling the shop not to order. Linn, a new persona in a separate workspace, independently reached the same disposition. There is good digital placement evidence, but no supplier confirmation that the buyer will only have to confirm the placements. The connector hole size and missing paste plots also matter to this particular order.

## Who actually did the work?

In the packaged desktop it selected **New brief from edition…** and addressed **Cleo alone**. The [registered protocol](../examples/inverter-peer-return/protocol.json) specified one engineering exchange and one fresh intake after it settled. No operator repaired a file, invited Arden, or sent the participants subsequent findings.

Cleo retrieved its own exact verdict and authored [a 3,188-byte brief for Arden](../examples/inverter-peer-return/cleo-to-arden.md). The `work.request` operation in `call-01b7d388ec4ab1a29d8a109db1cb0680` carried both the measured pad locations and the held edition. Arden’s first accepted input contained that invitation and the complete brief; `call-ee62d2a83bc36ded51a22f5692fc86e8` accepted the work. These are distinct facts: sending a brief does not prove that it reached a decision, and receiving it does not mean the invitee agreed to act. [Both ends in Studio](assets/studio-brief-receipts.jpg)

Across **42** exchanges there were 22 messages from Cleo, 15 returned communications, one acceptance and four returned ZIP editions. All four delivery notices reached a Cleo decision. Cleo measured a 1.645 mm discrepancy between the provisional regulator land patterns. Arden changed the footprint, routing and placement, then added the scope of the supporting evidence. When a receiving command could not open a checker in the designer’s directory, the peers diagnosed that check failure themselves. Neither treated it as a board failure.

### What we know about placement zero

The independent workstation imported only public reference cells into **EasyEDA Standard 6.5.61** and exported their placements in Projects Offline mode. The [documented Mid, Ref and Pad columns](https://docs.easyeda.com/en/PCB/Export-Coordinate/) distinguish midpoint, footprint origin and pin one. For all 14 exact catalog footprints in this test, Mid and Ref coincided to the export’s 0.01 mm precision; every exported pin-one coordinate matched the source. This includes the asymmetric C86206 regulator. The [original calibration export and replay](../examples/inverter-peer-return/placement-calibration/) are separate from the design workspace.

As a second comparison, we exercised the unmodified, [JLCPCB-recommended KiCad Fabrication Toolkit](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/how-to-generate-bom-and-centroid-files-from-kicad-8) on a separate copy of the issued board with automatic translations enabled. Its 18 centroids match within **0.000036 mm**; all sides and 16 angles agree, including U2/U3/U4. Its generic rules emit **180° for polarized C1** and **0° for the custom U1**, versus 0° and 270° in the issued CPL. Substituting those two comparison angles into the public footprint projection puts **0/2** and **1/9** centers on their matching copper; the issued entries give **2/2** and **9/9**. The [record](../examples/inverter-peer-return/placement-calibration/toolkit-comparison.json) supports the issued digital orientations over blindly applying a generic rule. Neither exporter observes the production machine. The buyer should not rotate either part on the strength of the comparison.

## Independent intake

Only after both engineering participants chose to stop, the shop created **Linn** in the packaged desktop and invited Linn from Cleo’s exact buyer edition. Linn had no past work and a general-purpose starting profile. Its separate project held only unmodified supplier reference material; the operator did not supply the prior diagnosis, a repair, a checker or the calibration. The delivered title and README do openly say **hold**: this was independent receipt of a real handoff, not a blinded classification exercise.

Linn chose **six** fresh runs on the original ZIP, checked exact part identities, inspected the actual order instructions and issued [a 4,611-byte buyer report](../examples/inverter-peer-return/independent-receiving-disposition.md) at event **1367**. Its [reply at 1371](../examples/inverter-peer-return/linn-to-buyer.md) says **HOLD**, with no claim that all the rotations are wrong. The report is a completed review; it does not release the hardware. [Report in Studio](assets/studio-inverter-linn-intake.jpg)

**One detail in Linn’s report is incomplete.** The first KiCad subprocesses reported no findings but exited 4 because they could not write reports under `/tmp`. Linn then ran them in a writable receiving directory: both exited **0**, saved their reports and printed zero findings. The [successful output](../examples/inverter-peer-return/linn-kicad-output.txt) reached Linn’s next accepted input. Its final report describes only the earlier failure. That reporting error does not change the order disposition. The original report is preserved as sent.
