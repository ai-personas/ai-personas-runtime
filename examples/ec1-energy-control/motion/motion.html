What does the return stroke cost?

Model and reset boundary

Use ε = 8.8541878128 × 10⁻¹² F/m for air, A = 0.0100 m². Ignore fringing, leakage, friction and inertia; motion is quasistatic. With gap g, C = εA/g. At the initial gap g₀, an ideal step Vₛ through any positive resistance to equilibrium gives Q = C₀Vₛ. The source delivers VₛQ; half becomes field energy and half heat. R controls timing, not that energy split. Disconnection at fixed gap exchanges no energy.

After isolation, Q stays fixed, V = Q/C and U = Q²/(2C) = Q²g/(2εA). Thus F = −∂U/∂g at fixed Q = −Q²/(2εA), independent of gap here, not the fixed-voltage force law applied at a constant 5 V. Closure work to the port is ∫F dg = U₀ − U₁. During the minute latched, force can remain nonzero but displacement and velocity are zero: no work or continuous power. Returning costs the port exactly its closing gain.

At the returned gap the plates are charged: another mechanical stroke needs no recharge but yields zero net work per round trip. To repeat from neutral, the last ledger row assumes a resistor placed across the returned plates; the remaining field energy becomes heat. A hypothetical reversible electrical recovery could instead reclaim that remaining energy, not a second closing gain. No practical losses or measurements are inferred.

What does this say about Exodus?

The closing stroke can drive a load: the field loses exactly what the ideal mechanical port receives. Keeping those proceeds does not pay for reopening the gap. At the latch there is still attraction, but no displacement in the minute held, so no work is delivered. Returning to the initial charged state needs no new charge in this ideal model; it needs the closing work back. Returning instead to the initial neutral state through a resistor releases the remaining field energy as heat. Starting again from neutral costs another charging step.

The linked EC1 workshop takes a different route: its fixed 1000 µF capacitor discharges into a resistive load. Its example trace is synthetic. There the load receives electrical energy as heat; here an isolated variable capacitor transfers field energy to a mechanical port. Neither open-circuit voltage nor static force is load power.

The workshop’s research/claim-and-energy.md distinguishes a reported propulsion force from electrical generation. The calculated force here is on one electrode; the anchored electrode experiences the opposite force, and its support does no work because it does not move. This is not net thrust on an isolated assembly. Nothing here measures, confirms, or refutes the reported Exodus thrust. Evaluating that claim would require independent force and momentum accounting; evaluating useful energy would require signed exchanges over a complete cycle, including reset.
